# 16 — Using AI honestly in coursework

*This kit is meant to help you learn faster. It is not meant to do your
coursework for you. A few rules keep you on the right side of that line.*

## 1. Your course's rules always win

Every university, department and even individual course can set its own AI
policy, and they differ a lot. Some allow AI freely, some only for checking, and
some not at all for certain assignments or any exam. **Read the rules for each
course** (the syllabus, course page or assignment brief). If they're unclear,
ask the teacher *before* you use AI. Nothing in this repo overrides those
rules.

## 2. Disclose AI use

If you used Claude for an assignment, say so, in the format your course asks
for. If it doesn't specify one, a short note works: which tool, what for
(checking, debugging, explaining a concept), and which parts are your own work.
Disclosing is never the problem. Hiding it is.

## 3. Use Claude to learn and check, not to submit unexplained answers

Good uses:
- explaining a concept or a step you're stuck on
- checking your own finished solution
- finding where your numbers and an independent calculation differ
- generating extra practice problems
- reviewing your code

Not OK: pasting the problem in, copying the answer out, and handing it in.

## 4. The workflow: try it yourself first, then compare

1. **Solve it yourself**, by hand or in your own code, even if you get stuck
   partway.
2. **Then** ask Claude to solve it independently, or to check your solution.
3. **Compare** the two. Where they differ, work out *why* and who is wrong.
   Sometimes it's Claude (see [docs/15](15-verify-ai-results.md)).
4. **Fix your own work** in your own words.

The template "Check my homework" in [docs/07](07-how-to-prompt-claude.md) is
built for exactly this.

## 5. Don't paste exam questions

Don't use AI during an exam or any graded test unless it is explicitly allowed,
and don't paste exam or test questions into it during or after the test. The
same goes for take-home exams, unless the instructions say AI is allowed.

## 6. Be able to explain every line you hand in

The test is simple: **if your teacher asked you to explain any line, equation
or number in your submission, could you?** If not, you're not ready to submit
it. Go back to step 4.

Also keep in mind that this repo saves your sessions locally to `solved/`
([docs/09](09-how-this-repo-learns.md)). That is a useful record of what you did
and how you used AI.
